Dr. Whitfield called me in March. His lease at the Airport Boulevard building
ran out in fourteen months and the landlord had already sent over a renewal at
$28 per square foot. He wanted to know if that was fair.

It was not. Comparable dental space in that submarket was trading between $22
and $24, and two buildings within a mile had vacancy. That gap was his
negotiating leverage, and he had no idea he was holding it.

We toured four buildings. One had raw land behind it the seller would carve off.
Another had landscaping costs buried in the CAM reconciliation that nobody had
questioned in six years. He renewed where he was, at $23.50, with nine months of
free rent and a $60,000 improvement allowance.

Here is what I want you to notice. The listing broker on that renewal represented
the landlord. If Dr. Whitfield had signed the first offer himself, that broker
would have collected both sides of the fee while representing the person across
the table from him. Nobody in that room would have been working for the tenant.

Market rents in Mobile moved about six percent last year. If your lease expires
in the next twenty-four months, the number on your renewal letter is a starting
position, not a price. Call me and I will pull the comps for your submarket.
