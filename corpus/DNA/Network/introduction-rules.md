## Hard blocks (never introduce, never co-invite to the same event)

- **HARD BLOCK — same category means competitors by default. Never introduce, and never co-invite to the same event, lender↔lender, CPA↔CPA, supply rep↔supply rep, GC↔GC, architect↔architect, or any other same-category pair. This holds even across different VERTICALS of the same category: a veterinary-only lender and a general healthcare lender still compete at the relationship level. Whitelisting a same-category pair requires Joe or Dell explicitly.** — taught by Joe Bookout, 2026-07-06 ("[No verbatim quote on record. ## Vertical awareness

- **VERTICAL MATCHING — vendors carry a Vertical inside their category (Bank of America has a veterinary-specific lender and a separate lender for all other healthcare). Match the vertical: a veterinary startup gets the vet-vertical lender, not the general one. Vertical fit outranks relationship stage when both are decent.** — taught by Joe Bookout, 2026-07-06 ("[No verbatim quote on record. Seeded 2026-07-06 in introduction-rules.md from Joe, verbally, that date; imported by ORDER 37.]")
- **A different Vertical inside the same category is NOT an exception to the same-category hard block. Two vendors in one category stay un-introducible however far apart their verticals are.** — taught by Joe Bookout, 2026-07-06 ("[No verbatim quote on record. Seeded 2026-07-06 in introduction-rules.md from Joe, verbally, that date; imported by ORDER 37.]")
- **STATE SPLITS — some vendors carve territory by STATE inside the same vertical. BofA vet lending: Michael Diehl = Alabama, Carlos Nieto = Florida. An Alabama vet client gets Diehl and a Florida vet client gets Nieto; sending the wrong-state banker wastes the introduction and steps on the right banker's turf. Check Territory and Vertical together when matching lenders.** — taught by Joe Bookout, 2026-07-06 ("[No verbatim quote on record. Taught by Joe 2026-07-06 (later that day) per introduction-rules.md's learned-rules log; imported by ORDER 37.]")

## Cross-category service overlaps

- **COMPETES WITH CARR — some attorneys, some consultants, and some practice brokers try to handle the real-estate brokerage side themselves. Never assume they will welcome a broker. Lead with "we take the RE work off your plate; you keep the client and your fee."** — taught by Joe Bookout, 2026-07-07 ("[No verbatim quote. Content originates in CARR corporate's "Vendor Info by Industry" PDF, which Joe provided and had logged on 2026-07-07; the rule is CARR corporate's, not Joe's own words. Authorship ambiguous — on the review list.]")
- **CROSS-CATEGORY OVERLAPS — some vendors quietly compete across category lines because their services overlap. Softer than the same-category hard block: these are defaults, not blocks. Two uses. (a) Do not introduce or co-invite two vendors who actually compete on a service even though their categories differ. (b) Engagement awareness, because a few overlap with CARR itself. Confirm the specific pairing before acting. The overlap map: consultants overlap with architects, CPAs, attorneys and financial planners; practice brokers with CPAs, attorneys, consultants and distributors who broker practices; CPAs with financial planners, consultants and occasionally IT; financial planners with insurance reps and some CPAs; architects with design/build GCs and with consultants or equipment reps that provide design services; IT companies with distributors selling computer equipment and with CPAs or vendors providing IT; general contractors with developers and landlords who self-perform construction; equipment reps' in-house financing mildly with lenders (most reps do not care how it is financed); insurance reps with other insurance reps and some financial planners. Full per-category approach reference: `vendor-by-industry-reference.md` (source PDF `CARR_VendorInfoByIndustry_source.pdf`).** — taught by Joe Bookout, 2026-07-07 ("[No verbatim quote. Content originates in CARR corporate's "Vendor Info by Industry" PDF, which Joe provided and had logged on 2026-07-07; the rule is CARR corporate's, not Joe's own words. Authorship ambiguous — on the review list.]")

## Delivery-weighted matching (the Practice OS steering rule)

- **DELIVERY-WEIGHTED MATCHING — when picking WHICH vendor to plug into a client's need, after the hard blocks and the vertical/state fit, prefer the higher delivery tier (deals.md Vendor Delivery Scoreboard). Delivery tier is a tiebreaker that sits BELOW hard blocks and vertical/state fit and ABOVE raw relationship stage: a vendor you are close to who burns clients is worse than a Solid one you are still building. The point is to protect the client experience and CARR's name — a bad introduction costs more than a missed one.** — taught by Joe Bookout, 2026-07-13 ("[No verbatim quote and no author stamped. Added to introduction-rules.md 2026-07-13 as "the Practice OS steering rule"; imported by ORDER 37 — authorship on the review list.]")
- **Never route a client through a ⛔ Benched vendor without Joe's explicit override, and log the reason whenever he overrides.** — taught by Joe Bookout, 2026-07-13 ("[No verbatim quote and no author stamped. Added to introduction-rules.md 2026-07-13 as "the Practice OS steering rule"; imported by ORDER 37 — authorship on the review list.]")
- **A ⚠ Watch vendor is fine to use — that is how a vendor earns a tier — but do not stack a fragile, high-stakes client on an unproven vendor when a Trusted option fits.** — taught by Joe Bookout, 2026-07-13 ("[No verbatim quote and no author stamped. Added to introduction-rules.md 2026-07-13 as "the Practice OS steering rule"; imported by ORDER 37 — authorship on the review list.]")

## Event guest-list rule

- **EVENT GUEST LISTS — before any joint event (happy hour, speaker panel, lunch-and-learn), run the invite list through the hard blocks. No same-category competitors on one list unless Joe explicitly decides otherwise for that event; big mixed events can be exceptions, but that is his call and never the default. Log every event in deals.md's Joint Events Log.** — taught by Joe Bookout, date not recorded in the source file ("[No verbatim quote, no date and no author stamped in introduction-rules.md. ## How the teaching loop works

1. The engine proposes intros (`engine.md` Section 1), already filtered through this file.
2. Joe or Dell approve, reject, or correct. A rejection gets ONE follow-up question:
   "what's the rule I should learn?"
3. The answer is captured with the `teach` verb, scope `intro_politics`, in the human's
   verbatim words, and activated with `activate-rule` on his yes. It appears here on the
   next compile. Do not edit this file to record it. Unconfirmed defaults are superseded
   by a confirmed rule, or retired, as they are ruled on.
4. Dell's onboarding (`DNA/Team/twin-system-playbook.md` Phase 3) includes a pairing-rules
   walk — his politics knowledge is deeper in some categories; capture it the same way.
