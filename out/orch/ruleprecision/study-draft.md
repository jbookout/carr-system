# Rule delivery precision study

Today's selector confuses a rule's subject with the action that makes it binding. An `Agent` call receives rules about red teams, Microsoft Copilot, architecture councils, and delegated disk sweeps even when the brief asks for one routine implementation. A prompt containing research or writing vocabulary can receive prospect-copy rules while asking for an internal engineering study. These are different conditions, flattened into shared tools and keywords.

The delivery paths accumulate candidates. They rarely reject a candidate using the full rule's conditions. A route's tool, verb, and command predicates are alternatives, and the route rail unions its answer with the compiled trigger table. Improving only one path leaves the other path's noise in the union. Compiled probabilities are estimates attached when a cue was authored, rather than probabilities calibrated for this turn. A top-five cap limits volume but does not make those five applicable.

The headline is mechanically reproducible: **71 applicable new JIT rules out of 262 delivered = 27.099% precision**, with **583 of 717 applicable rules available by the end of their turn = 81.311% availability**. The published boot is 60 shared full-text rules plus 11 personal full-text rules, and an index of 199 active rules. Corpus counts are not loaded full-text counts. The boot costs 125,442 characters, approximately 34,845 tokens under the report's estimator.

Some alleged noise is missing gold. A purpose-selected audit of 30 rule/turn pairs proposes 11 positive-label corrections. If independently accepted, today's corrected JIT precision is **82/262 = 31.298%**. That changes the interpretation of a few rules, but leaves 180 irrelevant deliveries. The original benchmark must remain the comparison denominator; the corrected audit is separate and cannot be used to tune this frozen test split.

## What the numbers do and do not count

The original simulation `a_today` unions the full boot, prompt selection, and **layered route-plus-trigger** tool selection. It is not the evaluator's default `system_moment`, which omits the route rail. Raw new-hook selections contain 92 TP and 238 FP. Sixty-eight selections repeat rules already fully booted: 21 TP and 47 FP. Excluding those repetitions yields the headline 71 TP and 191 FP. Excluding boot repetition is correct for new-rule precision; actual repetition still costs context tokens.

Availability credits a full statement that is assumed to remain in context, or a selected statement whose authenticated fetch is assumed to succeed. An index or one-line pointer is not full-text availability. The evaluation does not credit spontaneous index lookups. It does not prove that the agent obeyed a rule, that the hook ran in the actual host, that a transport cap exposed the entire response, that compaction retained the statement, or that it arrived before the specific action inside a multi-tool turn. The report's before-tools lower bound is 523/717 = 72.943%, versus its by-turn upper bound of 81.311%.

The historical 210-rule static corpus contains retired entries; the current live corpus contains 199. Four current rules lack a classification and are conservatively booted. Eight live statements differ from their static corpus version. Labels do not bind a statement revision, so a changed condition can invalidate a formerly reasonable label. Only 82 current rules have a positive test application; absence of a positive is not proof that the rule never applies.

`baseline_live_attempt` does not establish live Jev quality. Its diagnostic is `JevCallRefused: unattended_worker_off (rule_delivery_eval)`. The selector catches that refusal and falls back. An evaluator reporting zero outward exceptions is not evidence that a model judgment succeeded.

## Path diagnosis

The following figures include human and notification cases from all 72 test turns, keeping the evaluator's path-specific universes. They are not additive; overlap and boot exclusion produce the headline. `source-map.json` preserves the human-only figures, notification figures, per-path false-positive lists, and all 199 current rules' headline TP/FP/application counts.

| Delivery path | TP | FP | Precision | Why it produces noise or misses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Prompt compiled triggers | 12 | 64 | 15.79% | Positive keyword overlap, generic compiled always-on entries, and historical pack eligibility dominate; full action conditions are seldom tested. |
| PreToolUse compiled triggers | 17 | 54 | 23.94% | Agent/model-choice trigger bundles, command-family matching, and path triggers select related rules rather than each binding condition. |
| PreToolUse routes plus compiled triggers | 82 | 174 | 32.03% | The route rail adds useful action rules, but broad Agent routes and OR matching add unrelated rules; the union preserves table noise. |

Prompt delivery runs `ops/rule_trigger_delivery.py:advise`. It truncates a situation to 6,000 characters, allows five surfaced rules, and dedupes for two hours. Reviewed structural and prompt cues survive stale compiled text. Compiled triggered/residual entries require a non-stale entry and a matching cue, but accept cue probabilities starting at 0.50. Compiled always-on entries are included without a turn predicate. The compiler explicitly favors false positives: its source describes an extra paragraph as cheap and a missed rule as unacceptable. That tradeoff is the premise now contradicted by measurement.

The present Jev path uses a deterministic word-overlap shortlist of at most seven rules and one scoped batch judgment, under a 12-second budget. Its 0.8 score is a reading-list floor. **`judge_budgeted` returns an empty authoritative selection even after high-score answers**; it stores candidates in an advisory report and requires review. Consequently today's prompt route cannot be improved merely by changing a Jev prompt or raising its threshold. Comments in the hook still describe an older eight-request design. The operative code permits one request and does not authorize those candidates as binding.

`lib/rule_delivery_preuse.py:semantic_delivery` further limits prompt-selected rules to historical `pack` layer members with known packs. This is a separate classification from the new boot classes. A rule can be absent from today's full boot but rejected as old layer-zero/control guidance by this filter. The classifications should not silently substitute for each other.

`hooks/rule-pack-preuse-reselection.py` has ordered rails: UserPromptSubmit; exact top-level background boolean on Bash/functions.exec; then route-plus-table; then table alone. The scheduled rail requests every rule in its pack. The route rail fetches the union through one authenticated standing-context call and validates the receipt. `lib/rule_routes.py:route_matches` matches tool OR record verb OR Bash regex; path routes match globs and usually exclude read-only tools. A malformed route deliberately counts as a hit. This preserves delivery on configuration defects and should remain visible, but it cannot be mistaken for a high-confidence applicability decision.

Current routes dedupe by `(session, rule, tool)` for 30 minutes. Changing tools delivers the rule again. Prompt dedupe is independent, and it records selection before the authoritative text fetch has succeeded. Existing Claude dedupe compares entire receipt sets within a verified continuity generation, rather than individual rules; a changed set can repeat all its unchanged members. Codex behavior in existing fixtures still delivers on each matching call. These are token and availability concerns that an isolated case benchmark does not measure as session behavior.

The drift gate retains 11 pack regexes from the enforcement map. Its full source explains that the per-rule table was not substituted because of a granularity mismatch, byte-pinned observation fixtures, and the need to avoid moving an auditor together with the delivery mechanism it audits. It credits whole-transcript loaded-pack evidence and still has incomplete recognition of newer delivery receipt shapes. Its missing-pack count therefore is an observation with known blind spots, not a precision label.

The store's `standing-context` handler is the authority for active scope and text. Its default is gists, explicit `rule_ids` force full text, and `detail:boot` follows its own paged renderer. Pack declarations compute a monotonic layer plan; the shadow mode reports omissions without cutting the corpus. A gist-only pack declaration does not itself prove full text was read. The hook supplies both packs and explicit IDs, then renders only validated full statements.

Other consumers are not covered by this 72-turn headline. `tools/flash-run.py:pick_rules` uses the same advisory-only `judge_budgeted`, over the full static roster because Flash has no boot hooks. It currently returns no authoritative model picks, with a visible pending-review note. `tools/flash-prompt-rules.py` uses that function per interactive prompt. Its rendering truncates any admitted statement to 600 characters and five rules. This is a distinct truncated-text contract, and the proposed shadow hook does not change it. The room-bridge boot adapter carries explicit standing-context and overflow lookup instructions; it remains an independent boot consumer rather than another measured selector.

## Methods to test, with primary sources read in full

Structured predicates are the first candidate. Normalize hook event, tool name, executable command heads, exact record verbs, path writes, and the requested action. Match the full rule condition using conjunctions, with explicit exclusions for read-only tools, quoted command examples, internal-only copy, and routine implementation. Tool arguments are available before execution; the official hook guide demonstrates name matching, stdin tool input, and argument filtering. Complex Bash filtering in that guide remains best-effort, so our local parser should stay conservative and fixtures must cover quoted text and wrapper commands. This recommendation applies the available contract to this corpus; the guide does not measure CARR precision. [Anthropic hook guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks-guide).

Negative triggers should remove a proposed match only when an exclusion is actually expressed or structurally proven. Absence of a positive keyword is not a semantic negation. The current prompt table already has negative-pattern masking; the missing piece is rule-specific action scope applied after every delivery path has accumulated its candidates. Negation handling is a source-derived engineering proposal, not a result borrowed from a paper.

Use per-rule thresholds when a local classifier is necessary. Fitting a score and choosing an operational threshold are separate tasks. Optimize the constrained objective on TRAIN folds: maximize precision with availability at least 583/717, then minimize injected tokens. A single universal 0.5 threshold does not encode different rule costs or rarity. Do not fit a model and select its threshold against the same held-out observations. [Scikit-learn decision thresholds](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/classification_threshold.html).

Calibration must also use independent TRAIN folds. A score of 0.8 should mean approximately 80% positives among similar-scored decisions; an author-time probability does not demonstrate this property. With few positives per rule, sigmoid calibration or a simple empirical threshold is more credible than a flexible isotonic curve. A reliability plot and held-out precision are needed; a Brier score alone mixes discrimination and calibration. Most rules lack enough diverse positives to claim independently calibrated probabilities. [Scikit-learn calibration](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html).

A small local classifier is the structurally distinct alternative: sparse word/character features and structured action indicators, with one-vs-rest linear or naïve-Bayes scores for rule IDs. Fit vocabulary, IDF, model weights, and thresholds on TRAIN only. Group paraphrases and same-source sessions when creating folds, so repeated wording cannot leak across validation. Official documentation explains the sparse count/TF-IDF pipeline with naïve Bayes and linear classifiers; the proposed CARR multilabel/action features are our application of those components. The full official tutorial was read at a pinned revision because the current stable tutorial URL was unavailable. [Official text classification tutorial, scikit-learn 1.4.2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scikit-learn/scikit-learn/1.4.2/doc/tutorial/text_analytics/working_with_text_data.rst).

Two-stage retrieval is useful if the roster or text grows. Retrieve a generous shortlist using lexical search or bi-encoder embeddings, then score `(turn, full rule condition)` jointly with a reranker. Embeddings encode topical similarity; that is precisely the distinction causing noise here. An off-the-shelf passage relevance score cannot be called binding probability. A cross-encoder sees the pair together but costs more than independently cached vectors. With only 199 rules, scoring a local sparse classifier over the full roster may be simpler and avoid retrieval recall loss. These tradeoffs are an inference from the documented architectures, not measured model results for CARR. [Retrieve and rerank](https://www.sbert.net/examples/sentence_transformer/applications/retrieve_rerank/README.html), [Cross-encoder applications](https://www.sbert.net/examples/cross_encoder/applications/README.html).

Deduplicate statements already proven present in context before charging new text. Use rule ID plus current statement digest and verified context generation, not tool name or receipt-set identity. A once-per-rule/session proposal ledger can measure the secondary objective, but cannot claim a proposal was injected. If compaction loses text, permanent session suppression would reduce availability; recovery must be measured separately. The official hook guide documents SessionStart on compaction and accumulating additionalContext. These facts support epoch-aware context accounting, not an assumption that every compaction preserves rules. [Anthropic hook guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks-guide).

One-line pointers reduce immediate payload. They only retain availability if a measured follow-up fetch supplies the full statement before acting. Current overflow receipts already distinguish pointers from full text, and boot instructions prohibit treating summaries as the rule itself. Compare pointer bytes and lookup latency, then count full-text availability separately; do not manufacture an 80% result by relabeling a pointer as delivered authority.

Model-based reranking, if attempted later, must use existing Jev admission and count every admitted or refused request toward the hard job cap as specified by the root ledger. No paid Claude API is needed for the deterministic or local statistical designs. This study ran no model-based selector calls.

## Design the interface twice

| Design | Decision mechanism | Advantages | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per-rule action predicate registry | Pure `select(payload, baseline_ids, boot_ids, config)` over normalized tool/verb/path/action facts. Source conditions determine AND/exclusion logic. | Deterministic, inspectable, low latency, no provider or learned-vocabulary dependency. Directly attacks route overbreadth and quoted-command false matches. | Prompt intent needs carefully scoped predicates; long-tail rules remain unchanged unless separately specified. The first TRAIN candidate did not reach the availability floor. |
| Calibrated local classifier | Sparse word/character features plus structured facts; TRAIN-only per-rule scoring/thresholds; source binding for the live roster. | Covers paraphrases and adds applicable rules absent from today's routes. No external cognition cost at inference. | Small and paraphrased labels can overfit, rare rules cannot be calibrated convincingly, and changed live text invalidates learned assumptions. Requires held-out real turns and label-revision evidence. |

The shared seam should keep current delivery as one callable and observe its returned receipt beside a pure candidate. The earlier predicate snapshot scored 142 TP/347 FP = 29.039% and 1,355/1,690 = 80.178% availability on TRAIN. The current predicate module scores 140 TP/336 FP = 29.412% and 1,353/1,690 = 80.059% availability on TRAIN. Both snapshots fall below the 81.311% floor and were rejected. TRAIN baseline was 141 TP/427 FP = 24.824%, with 1,354/1,690 = 80.118%. The completed TRAIN calibration retained 17 supported rules using empirical accept/reject gates; no statistical classifier was fitted. The frozen winner scored 179 TP/41 FP (81.364%) and 1,392/1,690 availability (82.367%) on TRAIN. After the checkpoint, each held-out candidate was scored once: fixed precision 82.292% at 82.427% availability; real-sample precision 100% at 92.857% availability. The real sample covers engineering/governance and its new positives all concern retrieval. See final.html for the before/after table and limits. No post-test tuning occurred.

The implemented wrapper leaves `_process_today` responsible for all authority, fetching, cap fitting, failures, and output. It executes once. With `CARR_RULEPRECISION_SHADOW=1`, a local observer hashes input and session identity, extracts today's full-text versus pointer IDs, proposes candidate IDs, and records a locked per-rule/session proposal ledger. It logs current corpus/boot snapshot provenance and explicitly calls the boot basis a configured snapshot, not a verified session receipt. It never logs prompt, tool arguments, rule text, client paths, or exception text. Malformed config yields a fixed error. Any observation failure returns today's exact original object. Shadow is off by default.

The shadow record distinguishes `candidate_ids` from deduped `proposed_new_ids`, and sets `proposal_only:true`. Full selection supports availability scoring; new proposals support token estimates. Neither claims a delivered statement. A live precision proxy requires independently audited `(input hash, selector digest, gold, judged rules, verified boot)` rows. Selector scores cannot label themselves. Missing audited evidence must produce UNKNOWN rather than a green health claim. The root owns the health row and its threshold/loop bound action.
